PARAMETER: LDL Cholesterol ("Bad" Cholesterol)

WHAT IT IS:
LDL (Low-Density Lipoprotein) carries cholesterol to cells but can build up in artery walls when in excess.

PURPOSE OF THE TEST:
Measured as part of a lipid panel to assess cardiovascular risk.

GENERAL REFERENCE RANGE (adults, approximate):
Below 100 mg/dL is generally considered optimal; ranges vary by individual risk factors.

WHAT LOW VALUES CAN SOMETIMES INDICATE:
Very low LDL is generally not concerning and can sometimes reflect diet, medication, or genetics.

WHAT HIGH VALUES CAN SOMETIMES INDICATE:
High LDL can sometimes be associated with diet, genetics, sedentary lifestyle, or other conditions, and may relate to increased risk of artery plaque buildup.

IMPORTANT CAVEATS:
Reference ranges vary between laboratories, individual risk factors, and guidelines. LDL targets often depend on a person's overall cardiovascular risk profile.
