PARAMETER: Hemoglobin (Hb)

WHAT IT IS:
Hemoglobin is a protein inside red blood cells that carries oxygen from the lungs to the rest of the body and carries carbon dioxide back to the lungs.

PURPOSE OF THE TEST:
It is measured as part of a Complete Blood Count (CBC) to check for anemia, blood disorders, or general oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood.

GENERAL REFERENCE RANGE (adults, approximate):
- Men: 13.5 to 17.5 g/dL
- Women: 12.0 to 15.5 g/dL
Ranges vary by lab, age, sex, and altitude.

WHAT LOW VALUES CAN SOMETIMES INDICATE:
Low hemoglobin (anemia) can sometimes be associated with iron deficiency, vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, chronic disease, blood loss, or bone marrow problems.

WHAT HIGH VALUES CAN SOMETIMES INDICATE:
High hemoglobin can sometimes be associated with dehydration, living at high altitude, smoking, lung disease, or certain bone marrow conditions.

IMPORTANT CAVEATS:
Reference ranges vary between laboratories and individuals. A single number cannot diagnose a condition. Always interpret alongside other test results and a doctor's evaluation.
