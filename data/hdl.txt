PARAMETER: HDL Cholesterol ("Good" Cholesterol)

WHAT IT IS:
HDL (High-Density Lipoprotein) helps remove excess cholesterol from the bloodstream and transport it to the liver.

PURPOSE OF THE TEST:
Measured as part of a lipid panel to assess cardiovascular risk.

GENERAL REFERENCE RANGE (adults, approximate):
Above 40 mg/dL (men) or above 50 mg/dL (women) is generally considered favorable; higher is generally better.

WHAT LOW VALUES CAN SOMETIMES INDICATE:
Low HDL can sometimes be associated with sedentary lifestyle, smoking, obesity, or certain genetic factors, and may relate to increased cardiovascular risk.

WHAT HIGH VALUES CAN SOMETIMES INDICATE:
High HDL is generally considered protective for heart health, though extremely high values are being studied further.

IMPORTANT CAVEATS:
Reference ranges vary between laboratories and individuals. HDL should be interpreted alongside LDL, triglycerides, and total cholesterol.
